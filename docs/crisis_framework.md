# JKH Crisis Management Framework
For the John Keells Crisis Command Centre

## 1. Context: Why This Framework Matters for John Keells Holdings

John Keells Holdings PLC is one of Sri Lanka’s most significant corporate groups and is widely recognised as the largest listed conglomerate on the Colombo Stock Exchange. The Group operates across multiple sectors that touch everyday life, including retail, leisure, consumer foods, property, transportation, financial services, IT and other strategic businesses. Its scale, public visibility and stakeholder footprint make reputation management a Group-level business priority, not only a communications function.

The relevance of a crisis framework for JKH is therefore high. A reputational issue affecting one brand, business unit or sector can quickly escalate into wider public scrutiny of the Group. For example, a customer complaint at a Keells outlet, a product quality concern involving Elephant House, a guest safety issue at Cinnamon, a customs-related issue involving BYD, a data privacy concern, a workplace incident, or a regulatory matter can move rapidly from an operational issue to a public trust issue.

JKH’s own reporting places emphasis on governance, risk, stakeholder communication, ESG reporting and long-term value creation. This means the Crisis Command Centre should not function merely as a statement generator. It should operate as a structured crisis management system that helps the Group detect issues early, classify risk, activate the right teams, protect stakeholders, communicate responsibly and learn from every incident.

**The operating logic of the JKH Crisis Command Centre should be:**
`Signal → Diagnosis → Strategy → Action → Recovery → Learning`

This reflects the broader product concept from the earlier Crisis Operating System document, where the platform is positioned as an AI-powered command centre that supports detection, diagnosis, strategy, action, recovery and learning across the full crisis lifecycle.

## 2. Classification Framework

The JKH Crisis Command Centre should use a two-layer classification model. The first layer keeps the framework simple for users:

| Category | Description | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A. Issue** | A potential problem that can be handled within normal business operations. Low public visibility and limited stakeholder impact. | A single customer complaint at a Keells outlet, minor hotel service delay, small internal operational matter, or low-reach social media comment. |
| **B. Risk** | A situation with potential to create significant reputational, operational, legal, regulatory or stakeholder impact if not managed early. | Negative post about JKH brands gaining attention; unresolved customer complaint shared online; employee concern becoming public; regulator matter monitoring. |
| **C. Crisis** | A high-impact event that threatens JKH’s reputation, operations, stakeholders, legal position, or licence to operate. Requires immediate response. | Product safety allegation, guest injury, workplace accident, data concern, regulator inquiry, leadership controversy, cyber incident, national boycott. |

## 3. Crisis Severity Framework

Once an issue is classified as a Risk or Crisis, the system should assign a severity level.

| Level | Type | Meaning | JKH Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Level 1** | Issue | Low public visibility and manageable internally. | Single customer complaint at Keells, minor service delay at a hotel. |
| **Level 2** | Emerging Risk | Public signs appearing; issue requires monitoring. | Negative social media post about Keells/Elephant House/Cinnamon/BYD gaining attention. |
| **Level 3** | Active Crisis | Media, social media or stakeholder attention is active. | Product quality allegation, hotel guest complaint, port service disruption, customer data concern. |
| **Level 4** | Severe Crisis | Legal, safety, regulatory, leadership or national attention involved. | Food contamination allegation, serious workplace injury, guest safety incident, data breach. |
| **Level 5** | Enterprise Crisis | Threatens licence, leadership, valuation, or long-term Group reputation. | Fatal incident, major cyberattack, governance scandal, large-scale regulatory investigation, national boycott. |

## 4. Eight Stages of the Crisis Lifecycle

Every issue, risk or crisis should move through a defined lifecycle.

| Stage | Purpose | Product Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Prevention** | Reduce likelihood of crisis before it occurs. | Maintain playbooks, risk registers, training, mock drills, stakeholder maps. |
| **2. Detection** | Identify early warning signs. | Capture social media mentions, news alerts, complaints, regulator signals, internal reports. |
| **3. Validation** | Confirm whether the issue is real, exaggerated, or false. | Request evidence, affected location, screenshots, documents, business unit inputs. |
| **4. Classification** | Determine category and assign severity. | Apply classification model. Recommend monitoring, escalation, or full activation. |
| **5. Activation** | Engage the relevant Crisis Management Team. | Create live crisis room with team members, task board, decision log, and evidence vault. |
| **6. Response** | Execute communication and operational plan. | Generate action plans, holding statements, FAQ, regulator notes, media Q&As. |
| **7. Stabilisation** | Bring crisis under control and rebuild trust. | Track sentiment, media coverage, task completion, corrective actions. |
| **8. Learning Review** | Improve future readiness. | Produce post-crisis report, update playbooks, refine escalation rules and training. |

## 5. Crisis Diagnosis Framework

The platform should first diagnose the situation before producing statements. The AI should ask:

*   What happened? Who is affected?
*   Is anyone harmed? Is there a safety, legal, or regulatory issue?
*   Is the matter public or private? Is media/social media aware?
*   Are employees, customers, or guests involved?
*   Is the issue local, national, or international?
*   Is there visual evidence? Is there misinformation?
*   Is leadership implicated? History of similar issues?
*   Are investors, regulators, or authorities likely to be concerned?

**The platform should then produce a structured crisis brief:**
`Issue summary` | `Crisis type` | `Severity level` | `Affected stakeholders` | `Known facts` | `Unverified claims` | `Missing information` | `Immediate risks` | `Recommended playbook` | `Required CMT roles` | `Required approvals` | `First response recommendation`

## 6. Crisis Risk Scoring Model

Assign a risk score using factors (1-5 each):

1.  **Severity**: Harm, disruption, or reputational impact.
2.  **Velocity**: Speed of issue spread.
3.  **Visibility**: Private, social, mainstream media, or regulatory.
4.  **Credibility**: Supported by evidence or credible sources.
5.  **Stakeholder Impact**: Number and importance of affected groups.
6.  **Legal / Regulatory Risk**: Involvement of authorities/compliance.
7.  **Emotional Intensity**: Anger, fear, grief, or moral outrage.
8.  **Misinformation Risk**: False or manipulated claims.
9.  **Business Impact**: Operations, revenue, valuation, or continuity.

**Suggested scoring:**
*   **0–10**: Monitor
*   **11–20**: Issue Management
*   **21–30**: Emerging Crisis
*   **31–40**: Active Crisis
*   **41+**: Severe / Enterprise Crisis

## 7. Stakeholder Priority Framework

Prioritisation is dynamic based on:
*   **Who is harmed**: Customers, employees, guests, or communities.
*   **Who has power**: Regulators, Board, investors, or law enforcement.
*   **Who can escalate**: Journalists, influencers, activists, or unions.
*   **Who needs reassurance/info**: Employees, partners, media, or frontline staff.
*   **Who can influence opinion**: Analysts, community leaders, or advocacy groups.
*   **Who can impose penalties**: Courts, CSE, or government agencies.

### JKH Stakeholder Map
| Stakeholder Group | Likely Concern | Communication Need |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Customers / Guests** | Safety, quality, refunds, trust. | FAQs, hotline scripts, direct updates. |
| **Employees** | Safety, security, internal clarity. | Internal memo, manager briefing, HR guidance. |
| **Media** | Facts, accountability, public interest. | Holding statement, media Q&A, spokesperson briefing. |
| **Regulators** | Compliance, corrective action. | Formal report, regulator note, evidence pack. |
| **Investors** | Financial impact, governance, disclosure. | Investor update, CSE disclosure, analyst briefing. |
| **Board / GEC** | Risk exposure, decisions, accountability. | Crisis dashboard, Board memo, decision log. |
| **Suppliers / Partners** | Continuity, liability, disruption. | Partner advisory, supplier notice, updates. |
| **Communities** | Environmental, social, ethical impact. | Community statement, engagement plan. |
| **Government** | Public safety, national impact. | Official briefing, legal response. |

## 8. Communication Protocols

*   **A. One Voice Policy**: Only designated spokespeople may speak.
*   **B. Confirmed Facts First**: Separate facts, unverified claims, allegations, and internal-only info.
*   **C. Responsibility Language Matrix**: Use empathetic, factual, clear, and action-oriented language. Avoid over/under-apologising, blame-shifting, or speculation.
*   **D. Human Approval Rule**: No AI content published directly. Human-led final publication.
*   **E. Stakeholder-Specific**: Tailor detail, tone, and timing for each audience.

## 9. Message Strategy Framework

| Message Posture | When to Use | JKH Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Acknowledge & Investigate** | Facts unclear, but public concern exists. | Video alleging poor product quality at Keells. |
| **Apologise & Correct** | Company clearly at fault or service failed. | Verified service failure affecting hotel guests. |
| **Clarify Misinformation** | False or misleading claims spreading. | Manipulated screenshot falsely claiming illegal act. |
| **Defend with Evidence** | Allegation inaccurate; evidence available. | False claim about pricing or regulatory compliance. |
| **Pause & Verify** | Sensitive issue; facts incomplete. | Legal, safety, or data-related allegation. |
| **Cooperate with Authorities** | Regulators, police, or CSE involved. | Data breach, workplace accident, financial matter. |
| **Express Sympathy** | People injured or emotionally affected. | Guest injury, employee accident, community impact. |
| **Announce Corrective Action** | Fix or process improvement identified. | Product withdrawal, safety inspection, system patch. |
| **Distance with Accountability** | Misconduct involves individual/partner. | Employee misconduct or contractor negligence. |
| **Take Responsibility** | Organisation accepts accountability. | Confirmed quality failure or operational lapse. |
| **Explain Operational Facts** | Misunderstanding from lack of context. | Port disruption, property delay, supply chain matter. |

## 10. Crisis Management Team (CMT) Activation

### Required Roles by Crisis Type
*   **Cyberattack**: CEO (if severe), CIO, Legal, DPO, Comms, BU Head.
*   **Employee Misconduct**: HR, Legal, Comms, Operations, Sector Head.
*   **Product Safety**: Operations, QA, Supply Chain, Legal, Comms, Sector Head.
*   **Financial Scandal**: CFO, Legal/Secretarial, Investor Relations, Comms, CEO.
*   **Protest / Activism**: Security, Comms, Legal, Govt Affairs, Sustainability.
*   **Hotel Guest Incident**: GM, Leisure Sector Head, Security, Legal, Comms.
*   **Port / Logistics**: Transportation Sector Head, Operations, Legal, Comms.
*   **Environmental / EHS**: EHS Lead, Sustainability, Operations, Legal, Comms.
*   **Property / Construction**: Property Sector Head, Project Lead, Legal, Comms.
*   **Leadership / Governance**: CEO, Legal/Secretarial, Board Liaison, ERM, Comms.

### Severity-Based Activation
*   **Level 1**: BU owner handles and logs.
*   **Level 2**: Sector Head and Comms notified.
*   **Level 3**: Sector crisis team activated with Legal and Comms.
*   **Level 4**: Group Comms, Legal, ERM and GEC members join.
*   **Level 5**: CEO, Board Liaison, Investor Relations and senior Group leadership.

## 11. Approval Framework

| Crisis Asset | Approver |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Social reply** | Comms Lead |
| **Holding statement** | Comms + Legal |
| **Press statement** | Comms + Legal + relevant Sector Head |
| **CEO quote / message** | Chairperson-CEO + Legal + Comms |
| **Regulator letter** | Legal / Secretarial + responsible executive |
| **Employee memo** | HR + Comms |
| **Customer FAQ** | Customer Care + Comms + Legal (if sensitive) |
| **Investor update / CSE** | CFO + Investor Relations + Legal / Secretarial |
| **Media Q&A** | Comms + Legal + relevant business owner |
| **Apology statement** | Comms + Legal + Sector Head + CEO (if severe) |
| **Misinformation rebuttal** | Comms + Legal + Evidence Owner |

**Approval Rules:**
1.  No AI content published directly.
2.  Separate confirmed facts from allegations.
3.  Legal must approve high-risk statements.
4.  Investor-sensitive issues involve CFO/IR.
5.  Regulatory matters approved by Legal/Secretarial.
6.  Severe crises require senior leadership.
7.  All public statements version-controlled and logged.

## 12. Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

*   **Time to Detect (TTD)**: Onset to platform detection.
*   **Time to Classify (TTC)**: Detection to severity assignment.
*   **Time to Escalate (TTE)**: Classification to owner notification.
*   **Time to First Response (TTFR)**: Classification to first action/statement.
*   **Time to Approve (TTA)**: Time for stakeholder approval.
*   **Sentiment Stabilisation Time**: Time for negative sentiment to stabilise.
*   **Message Consistency Score**: Alignment of internal/external messages.
*   **Task Completion Rate**: % of actions completed on time.
*   **Post-Crisis Learning**: Completion of review and playbook updates.

## 13. Scenario A: BYD Safety Concerns & Reputation Support

**Challenge**: Public concern after accidents, safety claims, or negative social media narratives.
**Response**: **Customer Advocacy and Evidence-Led Reputation Support**. Use evidence to clarify facts and rebuild confidence.

**Action Plan:**
1.  **Verify Facts**: Vehicle condition, driver context, service history, location.
2.  **Holding Statement**: Acknowledge concern; confirm review.
3.  **Activate Team**: BYD BU lead, Comms, Legal, Tech Lead, Sector Head.
4.  **Monitor**: Social media, influencer posts, misinformation.
5.  **Evidence-Led Comms**: Factual safety info through experts and education content.
6.  **Owner Testimonials**: Verified testimonials as customer experience content (not defensive).
7.  **Correction**: Address inaccurate claims with evidence.

## 14. Scenario B: Customs Import Delays & Customer Education

**Challenge**: Uncertainty/delays due to customs restrictions or regulatory interpretations.
**Response**: **Transparent, Educational Communication**. Separate company responsibility from regulatory process.

**Action Plan:**
1.  **Customer FAQ**: Explain cause, expected process, and support channels.
2.  **Talking Points**: For sales, customer service, and dealer teams.
3.  **Media Explainers**: Podcasts/interviews explaining issues in simple language.
4.  **Direct Updates**: Regular updates even if no final resolution yet.
5.  **Clarify Control**: Distinguish what is company-controlled vs. regulatory.
6.  **Resolution Sharing**: Share progress and reassurance to restore confidence.

## 15. Crisis Command Centre Product Modules

1.  **Crisis Intake**: Input via text, screenshots, links, or internal reports.
2.  **AI Diagnosis Engine**: Classifies as Issue/Risk/Crisis; assigns severity.
3.  **Crisis Framework Library**: Stores playbooks for various scenarios (Cyber, Safety, etc.).
4.  **Listening Module**: Monitors social, news, and regulatory signals.
5.  **Crisis War Room**: Live workspace with task board, decision log, and approvals.
6.  **Stakeholder Command**: Maps affected groups, concerns, and response status.
7.  **Crisis Content Studio**: Generates drafts (Statements, FAQ, Memos) for approval.
8.  **Narrative Control**: Classifies claims (True, False, Misleading, Satire).
9.  **Evidence & Legal Vault**: Stores facts, versions, timestamps, and approvals.
10. **Recovery & Learning**: Generates post-crisis reports and updates playbooks.
